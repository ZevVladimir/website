A very interesting book that covers American history primarily through the lens of the native peoples here. I found it very interesting to see the details that even if I knew the general themes about how treaties were established and then settlers/the government would either renege or just ignore them. This was in large part a battle between who could make deals with Natives, who had the power to enforce them and even if they had power in writing could they actually do it on the ground. Especially in the first hundred years a lot of the expansion seems to have been not only government making treaties to get new territories but also settlers then forcing further and violating treaties with impunity. Overall, there is a message of how often Natives are just treated as some group of people that everyone isn't exactly sure how to deal with especially after genocide and forced assimilation don't fully "work" and natives still remain. But you have a history of these peoples that have constantly had their identities erased in many different ways but still have survived until today where they are maybe better off than before but still are generally living with higher rates of poverty, worse medical care, and worse opportunities. And overall there doesn't seem to be a very coherent thought process for what to do and so often other, foreign, conflicts or events take precedence in people's minds. I don't really know what my current thoughts are beyond that more should be done to help them achieve what they want and I'd be interested in reading more both about the history but also about current native people's ideas and aspirations.

I enjoyed this book, but I think most of it came from how it made me think about life and death and the purpose of life and death. Reading reviews online it seems that there are a decent amount of people who found the actual plot a bit contrived or predictable but maybe it's just how I read but I didn't notice/it didn't really bother me. I found it interesting to see Cyrus and how his life led him to think about the purpose of it all. I do think that the ending wasn't the best and I felt like I would've preferred for there to be more of a "realistic" or maybe more "harsh" ending where his mom wasn't still alive and he finds a different path to love. It does feel like a very fair tale or movie ending with how his love with Zee is presented and his realization of how he has mistreated him as well as the uncovering of the truth behind his mom. But I do think there is a bit more to it with the purpose of a life. And I think the book although it is centered on martyrdom and thus the purpose of a death I think manages to also connect back to that purpose of a life. There is a quote in there with something like a purposeful death requires a purposeful life. And the general idea of wanting to *do* something with your life and seeking the freedom to do it like his mom and Leila do for the brief week we see. And I think a lot of Cyrus' issues are from him seeing his parents' lives as not having a purpose and then just dying for no good reason. His belief that his mom was killed randomly and for what, but we then see she both did have a fulfilling life after but more importantly she had begun to discover a more fulfilling life before where she was more happy and in love. Emphasizing the life we do have is important.

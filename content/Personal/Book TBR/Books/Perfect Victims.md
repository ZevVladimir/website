An angry book, and one that demonstrates the right and need to speak and express that anger. I regularly would find myself if not denying at least casting doubt on what was being written or thinking of counterpoints and counterarguments. While I think this might be a reasonable thing to do, it is important to have a full context for things I think it is also important to note that the writing was very well cited and regardless of other context paint a damning picture. And the most poignant point to me is that why should Palestinians have to defend themselves at every turn and feel at risk of being perceived as terrorists or anti-semitic regardless of who they are. And the anger coming from someone who has lived and has had their family and friends live and die in a brutal regime and apartheid why would one expect them to be anything but angry. I found this to be very well written and presented and felt myself getting angry as well and feeling a shift in what I think. But I think there still lies other important facets that are present and as someone who is a third party with no blood connection to anyone in the region I find it important to be able to take the luxury and privilege of having that third party perspective and not just falling into a strong anger filled position that I think has happened for Palestinians and for many Israelis after almost a century of war. But its a thin line where one can not just ignore emotions of the people who are actually living through this hell and the people that are affected by the ideas and treaties encouraged by people from afar. 

This book has left me thinking, has left me angry, has left me further questioning my beliefs which I think is a sign of a very good book regardless of its content. It leaves me struggling to place my beliefs:
- A distinct worry for Jewish people (and a selfish one for myself since I have no faith that if I disavowed Israel or Judaism I would be permanently safe) and the corollary that they should have the right to self determination
- A belief that the Palestinians have just as strong right to the same self determination and governance for themselves. A right to be able to live lives not under an apartheid occupation
- A two state solution is unsatisfying to all and seems impossible given the current status where in the short term Israel has no pressure to agree to anything that it doesn't want militarily (the removal of Hezbollah/Hamas weakening of Iran) but in the long term the relationships and the strain if not complete destruction of them that Israel has with other nations and its identity as a state seem to me to be in dire jeopardy if not already doomed.
- In regards to people outside, in third party positions, it is right to criticize *some* things without context as you see them. E.g. the treatment of Palestinians in the West Bank/Gaza is not proportional and not necessary to the security of Israel. It has morphed beyond the justifiable for protection against attacks to a way of further settlement and abuse in an apartheid occupation. However, the complete disregard of context is dangerous and a way to reach viewpoints that are extreme and inaccurate but feel right

I enjoyed this and it's one of the books that follows a family's saga as they move and adjust to new lives in different places and how humans adjust and change. Similar feeling to [[Pachinko (National Book Award Finalist)]] or [[The Eighth Life]] although very unique in what it explores. This one was interesting in how each character really is just deeply traumatized, with the large traumatic events being out of their control and on the scale of geopolitics (primarily involving Korea: Japan's invasion, American control, and North Korea's dictatorship). We see how these people then grow up and primarily how they interact with their family and people around them. I think Anne stuck out particularly to me where she became separated from her husband and daughter because of MS, which they were not able to diagnose for many years leaving her stranded and separate. And then her growth and desire to take care of herself and how she found herself a new, and different life that she had some meaning in. Louisa was also interesting where she ends up having this really bad family relationship imo thats characterized by an extremely over-protective Dad who then just disappears at a very critical age and a mom who is dealing with a debilitating disease in a foreign country. And we see this resentment grow and fester and come out in many ways. But we also see towards the end her own growth and handling of these things when she marries someone who seems to be a good husband, and raises her own kids, and then most importantly finds her father again. The father's story was also interesting with an almost out of place movie worthy plot of being kidnapped and held in North Korea working in a concentration camp until he is able to escape to China where eventually his mind breaks down. The book isn't perfect but it was a very interesting read and one that I think handles the relationships between people well and is definitely something I'll be thinking about.

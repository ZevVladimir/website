# Hayashi Phase
## How long does it last?
- Fully convective proto-star
	- $T$ is constant
	- $L=4\pi R^{2}\sigma T^{4}\to L\propto R^{2}$
	- With $n=\frac{3}{2}$ $R\propto M$
### KH timescale
- $\tau_{\mathrm{KH}}\propto \frac{\mu^{2}}{RL}\propto \frac{1}{\mu}$
- So higher mass stars are on the hayashi track for a shorter period of time
- $\approx1\mathrm{Myr}$ for a $1M_{\odot}$ star
### Pre-main sequence lifetime
- Contraction is slowest when $R,L$ are smallest
	- More appropriate to use zero-age main sequence lifetime
- From Lamers
	- $R\propto M^{0.7}$
	- $L\propto M^{3.8}$
	- $\tau_{\mathrm{PMS}}\propto M^{-2.5}$
# Concept: Zero-age main sequence (ZAMS)
- After pre-main sequence stars are in HE and TE due to nuclear burning
## Example: Convective regions
- Three types of ZAMS star
	1. Completely convective $M<0.35M_{\odot}$
	2. Rad. core and convective envelope $0.35< \frac{M}{M_\odot}<1.2M_{\odot}$
	3. Convective core and rad. envelope $M> 1.2M_{\odot}$
- Schwarzchild criteria
	- Convection when $\nabla_{\mathrm{rad}}>\nabla_{\mathrm{ad}}$
	- Happens when $\kappa$ is large or $\frac{l}{m}$ is large
		- Needs a lot of energy flux to be transported
		- Latter condition happens for CNO cycle with large $\nu$
- Size of convective core increases with stellar mass
	- $\nabla_{\mathrm{ad}}$ decreases as radiation pressure is more important
- For lower mass stars
	- p-p chain has a smaller $\epsilon_{nuc}$ gradient 
	- So $\nabla_{\mathrm{rad}}$ is low 
	- Remains radiative
	- But $T$ drops in the envelope
		- $\kappa$ increases
		- Can lead to convection
- For the lowest mass stars
	- $\kappa$ is always high
	- Star is fully convective
	- Remain on their hayashi tracks

# Notes
## Abstract
- Edges in mass-period space for rocky planets can be explained by combination of tidal decay and photoevaporation
- The edges in the radius period space (rocky planet desert) is a signature of magnetic drag (from the stellar magnetic field) on the orbit 
## Main Paper
### Introduction
- There is a clear gap separating planets of size $\lesssim1.6-2R_{\oplus}$ and those of size $\sim2-4R_{\oplus}$ in radius period space
	- Smaller group is generally rocky terrestrial
		- Origin could be stripped cores of larger sub Neptunes
		- Or cores smaller than $\sim1-2M_{\oplus}$ could not accrete sufficient nebular gas to emerge as sub-Neptunes and are born rocky
	- Here consider the interplay between photoevaporation and tidal decay in creating a sharp cutoff to the maximum mass of short-period rocky planets
- Another edge is the hint of a desert at $\lesssim1-3$ days and $\lesssim1RP\oplus$ in radius period space
	- Can't be due to mass loss as planets are already rocky
	- Likely not due to tidal decay since edge populations are smaller than earth
	- Unlikely to be detection bias as small planets are detected at longer orbital periods
	- But many are around low mass stars that would be subject to strong stellar magnetic field leading to orbital decay from magnetic drag
### Discussion/Conclusion
- They have demonstrated how the radiative tidal and magnetic interactions between the host star and an orbiting planet sculpt the three observed edges of the short-period rocky planet population
- Limits derived from evaporation/primordial gap, tidal decay, and magnetic drag outline boundary of a region inside which surviving rocky planets are expected
#### Magnetic Drag, Disintegrating Planets, and Lava Planets
- Inside 1 day rocky planets $\lesssim1R_{\oplus}$ undergo significant orbital decay from magnetic interaction rather than tidal interaction
	- Different than hot Jupiters where tidal orbital decay dominates
- Could be the dominant mechanism to form disintegrating planets
- Could detect melting and active surface volcanism as well as non-thermal radio emission at the cyclotron frequency
#### Caveats and suggestions
- Outcome of photo-evaporative mass los is sensitive to the assumed evolution of XUV luminosity
- Tidal decay depends on assumed tidal quality parameter
- Efficacy of magnetic drag relies on the assumed time evolution of the stellar magnetic field strength and morphology

> Short-period planets provide ideal laboratories for testing star–planet interaction. Planets that are smaller than ∼2 R⊕ are considered to be largely rocky, either having been stripped of or never having acquired the gaseous envelope. Zooming in on this short-period rocky planet population, clear edges appear in the mass–period and radius–period space. Over ∼0.2–20 days and 0.09–1.42 M⊙, the maximum mass of the rocky planets stays below ∼10 M⊕ with a hint of decrease toward ≲1 day, ≳4 days, and ≲0.45 M⊙. In radius–period space, there is a relative deficit of ≲2 R⊕ planets inside ∼1 day. We demonstrate how the edges in the mass–period space can be explained by a combination of tidal decay and photoevaporation, whereas the rocky planet desert in the radius–period space is a signature of magnetic drag on the planet as it orbits within the stellar magnetic field. Currently observed catastrophically evaporating planets may have started their death spiral from ∼1 day with planets of mass up to ∼0.3 M⊕ under the magnetic drag. More discoveries and characterization of small planets around mid- to late M and A stars would be welcome to better constrain the stellar parameters critical in shaping the edges of rocky planet population, including their UV radiation history, tidal effects, and magnetic properties.

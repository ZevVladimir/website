# Abstract
- Use Nbody simulations to show that if $\mathrm{UMaIII/U1}$ is self gravitating could not survive MW tidal field longer than a single orbit $(\sim 0.4\mathrm{Gyr})$
	- Suggests system is stabilized by large amounts of DM
- If it formed at the center of a $\sim10^{9}M_{\odot}$ cuspy LCDM halo would have a velocity dispersion consistent with measured by [[smithDiscoveryFaintestKnown2024]]
# Introduction
- Overall abundance of ultrafaint dwarfs (UFD) reflects number of low mass DM halos that can have luminous galaxies
	- Constrains which models of DM are feasible
	- CDM expects many more low-mass halos than UFDs
- UFDs also probe structure of halos
	- CDM models predict cuspy density profiles which are much more resistant to tidal stripping
- UFDs also allow for studying heavy element enrichment 
- Argue that if $\mathrm{UMaIII/U1}$ is a star cluster then its average density would be comparable to the mean density of the galaxy at pericenter. So it would not survive long on its current orbit
# Methods
- Density profile of $\rho_{\star}(r)=\rho_{0}\exp(-r/r_{\star})$
	- $r_{\star}$ is the scale radius
	- $M_{\star}=8\pi \rho_{0}r_{\star}^{3}$
	- $r_{h}\approx2.67r_{\star}$ is the 3D half-mass radius
	- $R_{h}\approx2.02r_{\star}$ is the 2D half-mass radius
- System is inherently collisional
- They choose to model as collisionless
	- Missing out on binary stars and stellar remnants
	- Discussed in [[cernyNoObservationalEvidence2025]]
- Use $10^{6}$ particles in exponential sphere with isotropic velocity dispersion
- Assume static analytical potential for MW
- Use $\texttt{SUPERBOX}$
- Find model tidally disrupts in less than two $T_{\mathrm{orb}}=0.4\mathrm{Gyr}$
# Conclusion
- Orbit of system is well characterized
	- Pericentric distance $\approx13\mathrm{kcp}$
	- Apocentric distance $\approx30\mathrm{kpc}$
	- Radial orbital time $\approx0.4\mathrm{Gyr}$
- Simulations do not rule out tidal stirring which could have enhances $\sigma_{\mathrm{los}}$ to high enough that would have caused tidally stripped stars
- If confirmed it would have a very high dark matter density which would have strong implications for DM models (FDM and SIDM)

> The recently discovered stellar system Ursa Major III/UNIONS 1 (UMa3/U1) is the faintest known Milky Way satellite to date. With a stellar mass of ${16}_{-5}^{+6}\,{M}_{\odot }$ and a half-light radius of 3 ± 1 pc, it is either the darkest galaxy ever discovered or the faintest self-gravitating star cluster known to orbit the Galaxy. Its line-of-sight velocity dispersion suggests the presence of dark matter, although current measurements are inconclusive because of the unknown contribution to the dispersion of potential binary stars. We use N-body simulations to show that, if self-gravitating, the system could not survive in the Milky Way tidal field for much longer than a single orbit (roughly 0.4 Gyr), which strongly suggests that the system is stabilized by the presence of large amounts of dark matter. If UMa3/U1 formed at the center of a ∼109 M ⊙ cuspy LCDM halo, its velocity dispersion would be predicted to be of order ∼1 km s‑1. This is roughly consistent with the current estimate, which, neglecting binaries, places σ los in the range 1–4 km s‑1. Because of its dense cusp, such a halo should be able to survive the Milky Way tidal field, keeping UMa3/U1 relatively unscathed until the present time. This implies that UMa3/U1 is plausibly the faintest and densest dwarf galaxy satellite of the Milky Way, with important implications for alternative dark matter models and for the minimum halo mass threshold for luminous galaxy formation in the LCDM cosmology. Our results call for multi-epoch high-resolution spectroscopic follow-up to confirm the dark matter content of this extraordinary system.

## Main Paper
### Introduction
- Axion-like particles are appealing DM candidates
- If DM is composed of ultra-light particles ($m\ll \mathrm{eV}$) then their occupation number far exceeds unity
	- This means DM can be treated as a coherent classical field with wave phenomenal like interference
	- In this regime interference effect leads to $\mathcal{O}(1)$ fluctuations in the energy density of DM in virialized structures (e.g. DM halos)
- These density fluctuations source gravitational perturbations
	- This can perturb motions of stars in the DM halo potential
	- Stars initially have velocity dispersions far below the DM velocity dispersion
	- Gravitational fluctuations from DM wave interference can dynamically heat the motions of stars over time
	- Strongest in smallest galaxies as it grows with increasing de Broglie wavelength
- Why revisit bounds of ULDM
	- Constraints on DM mass before relied on approximate treatment of DM wave interference with linear perturbation theory
	- Recent suggestions that tidal stripping could weaken or invalidate prior constraints
	- Recent suggestions that DM self-interactions could significantly alter the bounds of the DM particle mass from ultra-faint dwarfs
	- Galaxies even smaller (micro-galaxies) have been discovered
## Ursa Major III/UNIONS 1
- [[devlinReevaluatingUMa3U12025a]] considers that UMa3/U1 is the remnant of a initially much more massive star cluster
	- Models start with $\approx6000$ stars
	- This would leave a stellar trail that should be prominent
- Also believed to have been accreted 
	- If it was a stellar cluster the galaxy it is a part of should also contribute to the stellar stream
- UMa3/U1 should give a strong lower limit on the mass $m\gtrsim10^{-17}\mathrm{eV}$
## Discussion/Conclusion
- Presented updated lower bounds on mass of DM particle
	- Using the kinematics of the smallest galaxies
	- Find $m>8\times10^{-18}\mathrm{eV}$ with $95\%$ confidence
- Bound is insensitive to uncertainties in baryonic physics
	- Feedback from star formation
	- Thermal history of the IGM
	- The galaxy-halo connection
- Robustness is because they do not rely on predictions for the central DM density but use empirically observed DM densities
	- With $M_{\mathrm{DM}}/M_{*}\approx600$ inside stellar half-light radius dynamics are simple to model
- Modeling is simplified by their results that the FDM heating in ultra-faint dwarfs should not be affected by tidal stripping
- From the constraint if DM is indeed composed of ultra-light particles it is indistinguishable from CDM using all existing probes of small-scale structure
## Questions
- When does a galaxy become a star cluster?
	- Age of the object and the stars inside
	- Dark matter concentration?
	- Indications of larger stars that were once there
- What exactly does fuzzy dark matter mean?

> The particle mass of dark matter (DM) was previously constrained using kinematics of ultra-faint dwarf galaxies to $m > 3 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{eV}$. This constraint, which excludes the "fuzzy" range of ultra-light dark matter from comprising all of the DM, relies on an estimate of the heating rate from fuzzy dark matter (FDM) wave interference using linear perturbation theory. Here, we compare the results of this perturbative calculation to full Schr\"odinger-Poisson simulations of the evolution of star particles in FDM halos. This comparison confirms theoretical expectations that FDM heating is stronger in fully nonlinear simulations due to the formation of a dense central soliton whose fluctuations enhance gravitational perturbations, and that bounds on the DM particle mass using this perturbative method are indeed conservative. We also show that these bounds are not affected by possible tidal stripping, since for dwarf satellites like Segue 1, the tidal radius is much larger than the observed size of the galaxy. We further show that the constraints on the mass cannot be evaded by invoking DM self-interactions, due to constraints on the self-interaction from large-scale structure. Lastly, we show that if the recently discovered system Ursa Major III/UNIONS I is a galaxy, the observed properties of this object strengthen the lower bound on the DM mass by over an order of magnitude, to $m > 8 \times 10^{-18}\,\mathrm{eV}$, at 95% confidence. This constraint could further be strengthened considerably by more precise measurements of the size and velocity dispersion of this and other similar galaxies, and by using full Schr\"odinger-Poisson simulations.

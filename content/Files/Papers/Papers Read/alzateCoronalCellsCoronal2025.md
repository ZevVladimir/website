## Abstract
- Analyze high-cadence high-resolution extreme ultraviolet images
- Show that, throughout the solar cycle, mid-latitude coronal holes (CHs) are made up of ubiquitous and space-filling funnel-shaped structures (cells) anchored to unipolar magnetic flux concentrations in network lanes
- Coronal cells and coronal plumes (in CHs) are a manifestation of ubiquitous cells
- Cell properties
	- Depend on magnetic field intensity at their footpoint and connectivity in the corona
		- Either close in opposite polarity regions (CF-cells)
		- Or extend to form open field (OF) cells
	  Lifetime mirrors that of magnetic flux concentrations revealed by magnetograms
		- Slowly emerge then disappear in a matter of a few hours to a few days
- Coronal cells contribute to the dynamics of CH boundary
## Main Paper
### Introduction
- Coronal cells are the result of closely spaced radial flame-like structures that appear as cells when seen from above and as plumes when seen on the limb
	- Bright centers are separated by narrow dark lanes
	- Centered above a concentration of photospheric flux in magnetogram observations
	- CF cells are found most commonly between filament channels and CH boundaries
- Small scale and transient magnetic activity might play a role in the formation and evolution of plumes
- Mid-latitude CHs are important for space weather as they are a source of recurrent high speed streams responsible for a key subset of geomagnetic events
- CH boundaries could also be a possible source of slow solar wind
### Results
- OF cells appear to be building blocks of CHs with their magnetic field expanding outward to form the sun open field
- Coronal cells affect CH-boundary dynamics
	- Affected by differential rotation and the emergence/decay of bipolar structures
### Discussion
- Observations show that cells are ubiquitous consequence of network magnetic flux concentrations within a large unipolar area both inside and outside CHs
- CH volume can be separated into
	- OF cells
	- Dark lanes delimiting OF cells
	- Occasional coronal bright points
- Region between OF cells and CF cells defines the CH boundary 
- Inside a CH cells appear with different levels of brightness dependent on the concentration of magnetic flux
- In principle coronal plumes could sustain the entire CH volume
- Emergence and decay of coronal cells changes mid-latitudes CH boundaries on size scale of the order of a supergranule in a few hours to a few days
#### Implications of "coronal cell" theory
- Each OF cell might be considered a unit of the open field region giving rise to solar wind. But this could be varied stemming from
	- A dark lane between 2 faint OF cells
	- A dark lane between a faint and a bright OF cell
	- A dark lane in which closed field plasma from a coronal bright point could be released forming a jet
	- A dark lane forming the CH boundary adjacent to a CF cell closing into nearby or far opposite polarity magnetic flux concentrations
	- A dark lane forming the CH boundary adjacent to a CF cell closing nearby active regions
- 
## Conclusion
- Provide morphological overview of coronal cells as an ubiquitous and persistent consequence of network magnetic field concentrations
- Both cells inside and outside mid-latitude CHs appear as cells confined by dark lanes when viewed on disk and as plumes when viewed close to sun limb
- Lifetime ranges from a few hours to a few days. Have size scales on the order of super granules and are always present throughout a solar cycle
- Cell's characteristics are related to properties of the photospheric magnetic flux in which they are rooted and subsequent magnetic connectivity in the corona
- Outward propagating jet-like features are present inside the CH and can be separated
	- Classic jets from bright OF-cells and/or coronal bright points
	- Persistent jet-like activity stemming from the cell's base with correspondence to the network field concentration
	- Occasional jet-like activity in dark lanes

> Using advanced processing techniques, we analyze high-cadence, high-resolution extreme ultraviolet images and show that, throughout the solar cycle, mid-latitude coronal holes (CHs) are made up of ubiquitous and space-filling funnel-shaped structures (or cells) anchored to unipolar magnetic flux concentrations in network lanes. We demonstrate that the coronal cells, previously documented in the magnetically closed regions, as well as coronal plumes, inside CHs, are a particular manifestation of ubiquitous cells. The cell properties depend on the magnetic field intensity at their footpoint and connectivity in the corona, either closing in opposite polarity regions (CF-cells) or extending to form open-field (OF-cells). The OF-cells reach size scales on the order of super-granules and are characterized by dark lanes delimiting ray-like features both showing, at different levels, persistent jet-like ejections. The cells' lifetime mirrors that of magnetic flux concentrations revealed by magnetograms, slowly emerging and then disappearing in a matter of a few hours to a few days in a one-to-one correspondence. When a cell forms along the CH boundary, it can alter the CH boundary by shrinking or expanding the CH by an approximately supergranular cell unit. Therefore, coronal cells can contribute to the dynamics of CH boundary. We contextualize these observations in a "coronal cell" theory potentially able to provide an explanation for fine scale coronal structures and jetting activity in polar coronal holes.

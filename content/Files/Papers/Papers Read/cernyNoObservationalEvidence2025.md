# Abstract
- Present new spectroscopic observation with $N=16$ members of UMaIII/U1
- With higher precision spectra find $95\%$ confidence level velocity dispersion limit of $\sigma_{v}<2.3\;\mathrm{km\;s^{-1}}$ 
	- Now no observational evidence for dark matter in the system
- Measure metallicities for 12 member stars
	- Find $\left[ \mathrm{Fe/H} \right]=-2.65\pm0.1\pm0.3$
	- With $\sigma_{\left[ \mathrm{Fe/H} \right]}<0.35\mathrm{\;dex}$
	- Properties are more consistent with a star cluster
	- Do not fully rule out dwarf galaxy
- With this interpretation $\mathrm{UMaIII/U1}$
	- One of the most metal-poor star clusters
	- Potentially first cluster stabilized by substantial population of unseen stellar remnants
# Introduction
- $\mathrm{UMaIII/U1}$ was discovered by [[smithDiscoveryFaintestKnown2024]]
	- Faintest ancient stellar population known in the universe
	- Very small size consistent with globular clusters
	- But initial spectroscopic observations found substantial velocity dispersion consistent with DM halo
	- [[erraniUrsaMajorIII2024]] provided dynamical models demonstrating that the system could have tidally disrupted within 2 orbital periods if it was a self gravitating cluster
		- Would then require DM to stabilize
- If confirmed to be a dwarf galaxy would allow for testing of
	-  Dark matter physics
		- Annihiliation
		- Decay
	- Tidal evolution of DM subhalos
	- Inner density profiles of DM subhalos
	- Constraints on allowed mass range of ultra-light DM particles
- [[devlinReevaluatingUMa3U12025a]] showed 
	- System's long term survival could have been facilitated by retention of substantial population of stellar remnants instead of DM
	- The high velocity dispersion could be explained in the stellar-only case if there were a large number of binaries
- [[rostami-shiraziDarkStarClusters2025]] demonstrated
	- Initial observations are consistent with self-gravitating "dark star cluster"
	- $\mathrm{UMaIII/U1}$ contains significant black hole population
# 2. No Observational Evidence for Dark Matter in $\mathrm{UMaIII/U1}$
- Use both frequentist and bayesian approaches to determine velocity dispersion
- Find an upper limit of $\sigma_{v}<2.3\mathrm{km\;s^{-1}}$
- Using $\sigma_{v}$ and the azimuthally average half-light radius $r_{1/2}=2.1$ find dynamical mass: $M_{1/2}\lesssim10670M_{\odot}$
- Assume enclosed luminosity $L_{V}=5.5L_{\odot}$
- Then $\dfrac{M}{L_{V}}\lesssim1940M_{\odot}L_{\odot}^{-1}$
- Then find even lower for the tighter $\sigma_{v}$ constraints
- Thus conclude that the dark matter content is smaller than [[smithDiscoveryFaintestKnown2024]]
- This does not rule out any existence of dark matter
# 3. No Evidence for a Large Stellar Metallicity Spread in $\mathrm{UMaIII/U1}$
- Find metallicities span from $-2.99\pm0.34\leq\left[ \mathrm{Fe/H} \right]\leq-2.27\pm0.34$
- Find $\mathrm{UMaIII/U1}$ is a low metallicity system with no evidence for large internal metallicity variations
	- Mean metallicity: $\left[ \mathrm{Fe/H} \right]=-2.65\pm0.1\pm0.3$
	- Upper limit of $\sigma_{\left[ \mathrm{Fe/H} \right]}<0.35\;\mathrm{dex}$
- Find $\mathrm{UMaIII/U1}$ is more chemically similar to globular clusters
# 4. Reclassifying $\mathrm{UMaIII/U1}$ as a Likely Star Cluster
- Galaxies are distinguished from star clusters by the presence of dark matter
- Identified by
	- Large mass-to-light ratios
	- Significant metallicity dispersions
- Presented here show that there is no longer positive observational evidence for a galaxy classification
- As a star cluster $\mathrm{UMaIII/U1}$ is 
	- The least luminous ancient star cluster discovered
	- Long-term stability might require $>50\%$ of the baryonic mass to be in unseen in some combination of
		- White dwarfs
		- Neutron stars
		- Black holes
	- Among the most metal poor cluster in the MW
- To further test they suggest
	- Seeking evidence of chemical abundance differences across member stars
	- Neutron-capture abundance measurements 
	- Seeking signatures of stellar mass loss to differentiate between
		- Dark matter dominated scenario of [[erraniUrsaMajorIII2024]]
		- Collisional stellar remnant dominated scenario of [[devlinReevaluatingUMa3U12025a]]

# Conclusion
- Present a second epoch of RV measurements for stars in $\mathrm{UMaIII/U1}$
- Results remove previous observational evidence for large velocity dispersion and DM content based on 
	- Confirmation and then exclusion of a stellar binary
	- Regression to the mean of member star velocities with higher precision measurements

> The extremely-low-luminosity, compact Milky Way satellite Ursa Major III / UNIONS 1 (UMaIII/U1; $L_V = 11 \ L_{\odot}$; $a_{1/2} = 3$ pc) was found to have a substantial velocity dispersion at the time of its discovery ($σ_v = 3.7^{+1.4}_{-1.0} \rm \ km \ s^{-1}$), suggesting that it might be an exceptional, highly dark-matter-dominated dwarf galaxy with very few stars. However, significant questions remained about the system's dark matter content and nature as a dwarf galaxy due to the small member sample ($N=11$), possible spectroscopic binaries, and the lack of any metallicity information. Here, we present new spectroscopic observations covering $N=16$ members that both dynamically and chemically test UMaIII/U1's true nature. From higher-precision Keck/DEIMOS spectra, we find a 95% confidence level velocity dispersion limit of $σ_v< 2.3 \rm \ km \ s^{-1}$, with a $\sim$120:1 likelihood ratio now favoring the expected stellar-only dispersion of $σ_* \approx 0.1 \rm \ km \ s^{-1}$ over the original $3.7 \rm \ km \ s^{-1}$ dispersion. There is now no observational evidence for dark matter in the system. From Keck/LRIS spectra targeting the Calcium II K line, we also measure the first metallicities for 12 member stars, finding a mean metallicity of $\rm [Fe/H] = -2.65 \; \pm \, 0.1$ (stat.) $\pm \,0.3$ (zeropoint) with a metallicity dispersion limit of $σ_{\rm [Fe/H]} < 0.35$ dex (at the 95% credible level). Together, these properties are more consistent with UMaIII/U1 being a star cluster, though the dwarf galaxy scenario is not fully ruled out. Under this interpretation, UMaIII/U1 ranks among the most metal-poor star clusters yet discovered and is potentially the first known example of a cluster stabilized by a substantial population of unseen stellar remnants.

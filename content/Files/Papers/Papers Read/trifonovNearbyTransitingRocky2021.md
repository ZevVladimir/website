# Notes
## Abstract
- Detected a transiting rocky planet around red dwarf star
	- Based on properties of the star and the planet's short orbital period/high equilibrium temperature the planet is suitable for emission and transit spectroscopy
- Determining atmospheric properties is possible using transmission and emission spectroscopy
## Main Paper
- With transit photometry and Doppler RV measurements can determine precise values for exoplanets of
	- Mass
	- Radius
	- Bulk densities
	- Surface gravities
- Determination of atmospheric properties is possible with transmission and emission spectroscopy
- For location of Gliese 486 b and knowing its mass and radius indicates that it has an iron-to-silicate ratio similar to Earth
	- $M=2.82M_{E}$
	- $R=1.31R_{E}$
- Density indicates massive terrestrial planet
- Equilibrium temperature of $T_{eq}=701 \pm 13K$ or slightly cooler than Venus
	- This makes it suitable for emission spectroscopy and phase curve studies to search for an atmosphere
## Conclusion

> [!Abstract]

>

> Spectroscopy of transiting exoplanets can be used to investigate their atmospheric properties and habitability. Combining radial velocity (RV) and transit data provides additional information on exoplanet physical properties. We detect a transiting rocky planet with an orbital period of 1.467 days around the nearby red dwarf star Gliese 486. The planet Gliese 486 b is 2.81 Earth masses and 1.31 Earth radii, with uncertainties of 5%, as determined from RV data and photometric light curves. The host star is at a distance of ~8.1 parsecs, has a J-band magnitude of ~7.2, and is observable from both hemispheres of Earth. On the basis of these properties and the planet's short orbital period and high equilibrium temperature, we show that this terrestrial planet is suitable for emission and transit spectroscopy.

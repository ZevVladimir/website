## Main Paper
### Introduction
- Cannot reconstruct initial conditions and formation history directly from planetary size ordering
	- Dynamical interactions change the initially formed systems during formation and after
- Discovered planetary properties could suffer (and likely do) from instrumental and human selection bias
- Currently observed hierarchical structure of a system likely depends on the formation environment of the system
	- Can't infer the exact properties of protoplanetary disk directly from the planetary composition
- Studies indicate that sizes of planets are not randomly distribution
	- Correlation between planetary radius and orbital period
	- Correlation between masses and orbital periods
	- Dependence between stellar metallicity and planetary properties
- Planetary properties might be linked to stellar properties which relate to protoplanetary disk properties or mass/stellar type and thus the location in the galaxy
### Physical Processes Sculpturing the Architecture and Ordering of Planetary Systems
#### Initial formation
- **Gravitational instability (GI) and/or turbulent fragmentation**
	- Direct collapse of disk material into giant planets primarily in outer regions
	- Less likely to play a dominant role in ordering of inner, rocky planets
- **Core accretion (CA)**
	- Planetesimals accumulate to form rocky cores which accrete gas if large enough
	- Larger planets form further from star due to increases material availability (particularly beyond snow line)
	- Stellar metallicity affects core accretion
		- Higher metallicity promotes faster core formation (potentially more and/or larger planets)
#### Planetary disk migration
- **Type I Migration**
	- Affects lower-mass planets embedded in the disk, experiencing torques that cause inward or outward migration
	- Depends on disks properties
- **Type II Migration**
	- More massive planets migrate at a rate governed by disk's viscous evolution
	- Differential migration rates can lead to resonant capture or scattering
#### Migration of Resonant Pairs
- Convergent migration leading to resonant capture and divergent migration avoiding resonance depends on the following
- **Mass ratio**
	- Convergent migration is favored in pairs with similar masses
	- Divergent migration more likely when inner planet is much more massive
- **Disk Properties**
	- Density and temperature profiles affect migration direction and rate
	- Steeper profiles favor convergence
- **Resonance location**
	- Closer in resonances are more prone to convergent migration
	- Due to stronger planet-disk interactions
- **Planet-Disk interaction**
	- More massive planets interact more strongly
	- Might lead to faster or runaway migration
#### Planet-Planet scattering
- Preferentially retains more massive planets while ejecting smaller ones
- Results in systems with fewer, more widely spaced planets
- Produce planets on eccentric orbits
- Excite inclinations
- Give rise to more massive planets due to mutual collisional growth
- Exchange between inner and outer planets ordering
#### Secular planet-planet interactions
- Occur on timescales longer than orbital periods 
- **Secular resonances**
	- Occur when precession frequencies of two planets orbits match
	- Can cause large oscillations in eccentricity and inclinations
- **von-Ziepel-Kozai-Lidov mechanism**
	- In hierarchical triple system cause large oscillations in eccentricity and inclinations
- **Secular chaos**
	- With three or more planets overlap of secular resonances can lead to chaotic evolution in eccentricities and inclinations
- On their own less likely to change planetary ordering
### Discussion
- Find a strong tendency for inner planets to be smaller than outer planets
	- Found in both the "full" and "de-biased" samples
- Size ordering more pronounced in inner planet pairs compared to outer pairs
- Find larger inner-to-outer planet size ratios for systems with larger period ratios
- Do not find strong dependence of size ratios for planet pairs in resonance
- Find strong metallicity dependence of both size ratios and period ratios of planetary pairs
- Period ratios and radii ratios of planets orbiting high-metallicity stars differ significantly from those orbiting low-metallicity stars
- Inherent inner system bias from transit surveys
- Potential influence of multiplicity bias and correlation between planet mass and orbital period should be considered
- Systems with more detectable planets are more likely to be nearly coplanar and not necessarily  representative of general population of planetary systems
- Future theoretical work #future-work 
	- Develop more sophisticated models that can explain the observed size orderings, formation of compact systems, and diversity of architectures
	- Consider interplay between core accretion, disk migration, planet planet scattering, long-term dyanmical evolution
## Conclusion
- Find non-random and non-trivial size ordering of planetary system that depends on 
	- System metallicity
	- Planet multiplicity
	- Overall planet sizes
- Tendency for inner planets to be smaller
- Variation across different system multiplicities
	- For systems with three planets the tendency for size ordering is observed but with more variation
- Dependence on stellar and planetary types
	- Might also arise from small statistics and observational biases 
- Impact of data completeness
	- Data incompleteness affects detection of smaller planets
- Potential influences of stellar metallicity
	- Planets around metal-rich stars may show different size hierarchies compared to those around metal-poor stars
- Dependence on ordering on period ratios
	- Find size-ratio distribution depends on the period ratios non-trivially
	- Trend for planetary systems with smaller inner-to-outer size ratios prefer smaller period ratios

> [!Abstract]

>

> We present a comprehensive analysis of planetary radii ordering within multi-planet systems, namely their ordinal position with respect to their size in a given system, utilizing data from the NASA Exoplanet Archive. In addition, we consider not only the ordinal positions but also the specific period ratios and radius ratios of planetary pairs in multi-planet systems. We explore various dependencies on stellar host type and metallicity, as well as planetary types, and explore the differences between planetary systems with different planet multiplicities and different planetary pairs in the same system. Focusing on Kepler systems with two to four planets, we account for observational biases and uncover a robust trend of smaller inner planets. This trend is particularly pronounced in inner pairs of three-planet systems and exhibits variations in stellar metallicity and planet multiplicity. Notably, we find that the distribution of inner-to-outer planet radii ratios depends on the system's metallicity, suggesting a link between initial conditions and the resulting system architecture. Interestingly, planet pairs in resonance do not exhibit significantly different size ratios compared to non-resonant pairs, challenging current theoretical expectations, again, possibly suggesting that initially resonant systems could have been later destabilized. Our findings align with planet formation and migration models where larger planets form farther out and migrate inward. Importantly, we emphasize the significance of planet ordering as a novel and crucial observable for constraining planet formation and evolution models. The observed patterns offer unique insights into the complex interplay of formation, migration, and dynamical interactions shaping planetary systems.

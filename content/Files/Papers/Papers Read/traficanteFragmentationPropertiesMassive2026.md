# Abstract
- Core masses are dense gas condensations that are the step before forming stars
- The distribution of core masses is described by the core mass function
	- Precursor to the stellar initial mass function
	- No observational constraints on core mass function
- Here study fragmentation and core-mass function in LMC
# Introduction
- Stars form across multiple scales
	- 10s of parsec molecular clouds
	- Parsec scale clumps
	- 0.1 parsec objects
	- Compact cores 500<R<4000 AU
- Important to understand the environment in how it shapes the IMF
	- Gas temperatures
	- Radiative feedback
	- Magnetic fields
	- Turbulence
	- Metallicity
- The CMF
	- At 0.1pc scales traces
		- Fragmentation of the parental cloud
		- The global mass distribution of the environment
	- At 1000-5000 AU
		- Acts as the immediate progenitor of the stellar IMF
		- Preserves the imprint of large-scale fragmentation and the environmental conditions
- Relation between CMF and IMF
	- Depends on the dynamical evolution of the cores and timescale of collapse
	- If time-evolving  then the measured CMF might not actually match the IMF
- Observations of MW show that CMF typically resembles the IMF in shape with a core-to-star efficiency factor
	- Might not be the case in more extreme environments
- Difficult to study CMF due to rarity and distance of young proto-clusters
	- Only able to see them with high res with NOEMA ALMA
	- 1000-2000 AU resolution

> The fragmentation properties of parsec- scale clumps play a fundamental role in shaping the dense gas condensations known as cores, the immediate progenitor of stars. The distribution of core masses, the so-called core mass function, is the precursor of the stellar initial mass function, which governs the distribution of stellar masses and, consequently, the evolution of galaxies. The stellar initial mass function is often described by a typical Salpeter-like slope, although deviations toward more top-heavy distributions have been reported in extreme environments, raising questions about its universality and about the physical connection between the two mass functions. To date, there are no observational constraints on the core mass function and its link to the initial mass function beyond the Milky Way.

# Abstract
- Modern simulations rely on feedback from AGN to quench massive galaxies
- Introduce RAFIKI simulations that vary mass loading of AGN-driven winds
- Separate efficiency of the two kinetic feedback modes
- Lower-velocity quasar type mode can't quench massive galaxies but regulates BH growth and star formation in intermediate mass galaxies
# Introduction
- AGN feedback is needed to reproduce 
	- Quenching of massive galaxies
	- Stellar mass distribution of massive galaxies
- Substantial uncertainty
	- Magnetohydrodynamic plasma in accretion disk
	- Mechanisms powering outflows
	- Energetics of outflows
	- How AGN feedback quenches galaxies
	- AGN feedback has to be treated as a sub-grid process requiring many free parameters
- Observationally AGN are split into radiative and kinetic
	- Radiative: high eddington ratios (quasars)
	- Kinetic: accretion disks fuel jets of materials
- New simulations use a dual mode behavior
	- Two pathways for AGN feedback depending on accretion rate of central SMBH
	- Generally there is a dominance of one mode shaping galaxy properties
- RAFIKI sets two different efficiencies for the wind and jet modes rather than just one
# Discussion/Conclusion
- RAFIKI suite consists of 20 runs varying two efficiency values (one for the wind model and one for higher velocity collimated outflows)
- Find quenching in high mass galaxies requires significant mass loading in higher velocity jets
- SIMBA-C's quenching mechanism is reliant on large scale heating of the CGM and ejection of gas to large radii rather than more internal process from heating of the ISM
- Effects of the wind mode
	- Regulates BH growth. Reduces quenching in massive systems
	- Impact star formation in intermediate mass galaxies
	- Creates variations in CGM conditions
- There is a fairly large degenerate parameter space for kinetic models of AGN feedback

> Modern cosmological simulations have now matured to the point of reproducing the evolution of realistic galaxy populations across cosmic time. These simulations rely on feedback from active galactic nuclei (AGN) to quench massive galaxies, yet the details of this process remain poorly understood. To address this issue, we introduce RAFIKI (Refining AGN Feedback In Kinetic Implementations), a novel suite of simulations built upon SIMBA-C that vary the mass loading of AGN-driven winds. Unlike the fiducial SIMBA-C simulation, RAFIKI separates the efficiencies of the two kinetic feedback modes, enabling a detailed study of their impact on galaxies, black holes, and the circumgalactic medium. We explore a range of galaxy and baryon properties in the RAFIKI runs and find that even with enhanced mass loading, the lower-velocity, quasar-type mode cannot quench massive galaxies. However, it plays a significant role in regulating black hole growth and star formation in intermediate-mass galaxies. We also uncover degeneracies in the parameter space that highlight the limited current constraints on AGN feedback. RAFIKI provides a controlled framework to disentangle these degeneracies using current and upcoming observations.

## Main Paper
### Introduction
- Looking to better understand the history of MW with looking at the structures that still exist today
- Have found that
	- MW's stellar halo contains contributions from multiple distinct sources
		- Material stripped from small satellites
		- Tidal debris from a single massive dwarf galaxy
		- Stars kicked out of the disk
	- The extension of the thick disk to low metallicities
- Obstacles to finding the precise timeline of the MW evolution
	- Absence of high quality age measurements for truly old stars
- Early stages of evolution
	- Gas accretion is highly chaotic
		- Leads to irregular highly turbulent and extended distribution of star forming gas and stars 
		- Strong stellar outflows and bursty star formation contribute as well
	- MW galaxies expected to accrete gas via supersonic narrow filamentary flows
- Slower accretion stage
	- Halo mass and virial temperature becomes large enough to sustain hot gaseous halo
	- Caused by cooling of gas is accompanied by virialization of gas in the inner regions
	- During this time thin stellar disks in spiral galaxies form

## Conclusion
- Separate low metallicity stars $([\mathrm{Fe/H}]\lesssim-0.9)$ into stars born in the MW proper (in situ) and stars created in low-mass dwarf galaxies and accreted onto the MW using the $[\mathrm{Al/Fe}]$ ratio
	- Production of aluminum in dwarf galaxies is suppressed compared to prolific enrichment in early MW
- Low metallicity $([\mathrm{Fe/H}]\lesssim-1.3)$ in-situ component is kinematically hot
	- Stars in this component reflect chaotic early pre-disk stages of MW
- Low metallicity stars exhibit large scatter in element abundances
	- Driven by strong variations in gas accretion and gas outflow rates and associated burstiness of star formation
- Nucleo-synthetic channels acting inside massive stellar agglomerations may have. contributed significantly to overall enrichment of early MW
- Median tangential velocity of in-situ stars increases sharply with increasing metallicity between $-1.3 < [\mathrm{Fe/H}]<-0.9$
	- Implies rapid formation of MW disk over 1-2Gyrs
	- During which the disk spins up
- Observations and theoretical models indicate violent head-on collision with the GS/E dwarf galaxy after formation of thick disk

> [!Abstract]

> We use accurate estimates of aluminium abundance provided as part of the APOGEE Data Release 17 and Gaia Early Data Release 3 astrometry to select a highly pure sample of stars with metallicity $-1.5\lesssim {\rm [Fe/H]}\lesssim 0.5$ born in-situ in the Milky Way proper. We show that the low-metallicity ([Fe/H]$\lesssim -1.3$) in-situ component that we dub Aurora is kinematically hot with an approximately isotropic velocity ellipsoid and a modest net rotation. Aurora stars exhibit large scatter in metallicity and in a number of element abundance ratios. The median tangential velocity of the in-situ stars increases sharply with increasing metallicity between [Fe/H]$=-1.3$ and $-0.9$, the transition that we call the spin-up. The observed and theoretically expected age-metallicity correlations imply that this increase reflects a rapid formation of the Milky Way disk over $\approx 1-2$ Gyrs. The transformation of the stellar kinematics as a function of [Fe/H] is accompanied by a qualitative change in chemical abundances: the scatter drops sharply once the Galaxy builds up a disk during later epochs corresponding to [Fe/H]$>-0.9$. Results of galaxy formation models presented in this and other recent studies strongly indicate that the trends observed in the Milky Way reflect generic processes during the early evolution of progenitors of MW-sized galaxies: a period of chaotic pre-disk evolution, when gas is accreted along cold narrow filaments and when stars are born in irregular configurations, and subsequent rapid disk formation. The latter signals formation of a stable hot gaseous halo around the MW progenitor, which changes the mode of gas accretion and allows development of coherently rotating disk.

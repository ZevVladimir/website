# Introduction
- Halo response to infall of massive satellites
	- Dynamical friction wake
		- Trails the orbit of the satellite
		- Depends on the properties of DM particle and model
	- Displacement of host's COM (collective response)
		- Inner halo is displaced relative to the outer halo
		- A large-scale dipole is formed
- Base function expansions ([[Basis Function Expansion|BFE]]) are used to quantify halo responses
	- They are a framework to decompose halo responses into harmonics and connect them to physical mechanisms
	- Useful to understand
		- Dynamics of galactic bars
		- Satellite-halo interaction
		- Response of galactic disks to internal/external perturbations
	- Also useful to reconstruct stellar streams without having to re-run simulations

# Conclusion
-  Examine halo response of MW like galaxies to LMC like satellites in a cosmological context
- Use [[Basis Function Expansion|BFE]] as a framework to analyze the response
	- Identify and characterize the evolution of host halo response
	- Decompose in terms of MW-LMC mass ratio, halo density center displacement, MW halo shape
- Findings are
	- Halo response is dominated by the dipole and the quadrupole
		- Dipole captures displacement induced by LMC-like satellite
		- Quadrupole reflects 
			- Host's initial halo shape
			- Formation of the dynamical friction wake
	- Dynamical friction wakes are present in all halos with LMC-like satellites
	- Evolution, amplitude and physical meaning of the dipole
		- Captures the response of the host to the LMC-like satellite
	- Quadrupole mainly characterizes the host halo triaxiality with an additional contribution from the dynamical friction wake
	- No strong correlation between density contrast of the dynamical friction wake and the collective response at satellite pericenter
	- Disentangling the present-day MW halo response to recover the pre-infall structure
		- MW initial structure encodes information about assembly history
		- Dipole contains information about MW halo density displacement set by the mass ratio
		- Quadrupole reflects pre-infall halo shape with additional contribution from dynamical friction wake

> The distribution of dark matter in the Milky Way (MW) is expected to exhibit a large-scale dynamical response to the recent infall of the LMC. This event produces a dynamical friction wake and shifts the MW's halo density center. The structure of this response encodes information about the LMC- MW mass ratio, the LMC's orbit, the MW halo's pre-infall structure and could provide constraints on dark matter physics. To extract this information, a method to separate these effects and recover the initial shape of the MW's halo is required. Here, we use basis function expansions to analyze the halo response in eighteen simulations of MW-LMC-like interactions from the MWest cosmological, dark-matter-only zoom-in simulations. The results show that mergers similar to the LMC consistently generate a significant dipole and a secondary quadrupole response in the halo. The dipole arises from the host density center displacement and halo distortions, and its amplitude scales as the square of the MW-LMC mass ratio, peaking 0.2-0.7 Gyr after the LMC's pericenter. The quadrupole's strength depends primarily on the original axis ratios of the host halo, though contributions from the dynamical friction wake cause it to peak less than 0.3 Gyr before pericenter. Future measurements of both the dipole and quadrupole imprints of the LMC's passage in the density of the MW's stellar halo should be able to disentangle these effects and provide insight into the initial structure of the MW's halo, the MW's response, and the mass of the LMC.

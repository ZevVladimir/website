# Abstract
- Introduce suite of 1,024 zoom in simulations of MW mass halos
	- Each simulation has unique initial conditions 
	- Unique combination of cosmological and astrophysical parameters
- Have weighting scheme for prioritizing regions of input parameter space that yield galaxies consistent with present day stellar mass - halo mass relation
- Find necessary to match significant events in the MW's pass to recover present day properties
	- Requires large statistical samples
# Introduction
- Want to compare the MW to other galaxies
- Find some rare features of MW compared to analogs
	- Stellar disk is more massive and compact than galaxies of comparable mass
	- SMBH in the. center is less massive than similar mass galaxies
	- Presence of a close, massive satellite and massive neighbor are relatively uncommon
- Zoom-in simulations provide statistical power with many galaxies and resolution for disks, bulges, and satellites
	- But still need a lot which is computationally challenging
	- Also inherent uncertainty in sub-grid models
- There is some uncertainty with the predictions from TNG due to it containing both physically and algorithmically motivated parameters that might not have uniquely constrained true/best values
- Here only vary 3 astrophysical parameters $(e_{w},\kappa_{w},\epsilon_{f,\mathrm{high}})$
	- Drive significant variability in stellar and AGN feedback
- Also vary two cosmological parameters $\Omega_{m}$ and $\sigma_{8}$
- Each hydrodynamic simulation has a corresponding N-body simulation

# Conclusion
- Introduce new simulation sweet of MW mass galaxies in $\Lambda \mathrm{CDM}$ cosmology
	- Each galaxy has unique initial conditions and varies certain astrophysical and cosmological parameters
	- Each also has an N-body counterpart
- Three most impactful parameters are SN energy, wind speed, and efficiency of AGN feedback
- Present weighting scheme that depends on how consistent galaxies generated with combination of parameters are with the observed present day SMHM
- Find a wide range of physical models can produce realistic galaxy populations
- MW-like merger history is very rare

> [!Abstract]

>

> We introduce a new suite of 1,024 cosmological and hydrodynamical zoom-in simulations of Milky Way-mass halos, run with Cold Dark Matter, as part of the DREAMS Project. Each simulation in the suite has a unique set of initial conditions and combination of cosmological and astrophysical parameters. The suite is designed to quantify theoretical uncertainties from halo-to-halo variance, as well as stellar and black hole feedback. We develop a novel weighting scheme that prioritizes regions of the input parameter space, yielding galaxies consistent with the observed present-day stellar mass--halo mass relation. The resulting galaxy population exhibits a wide diversity in structural properties that encompasses those of the actual Milky Way, providing a powerful statistical sample for galactic archaeology. To demonstrate the suite's scientific utility, we investigate the connection between a galaxy's merger history, focusing on Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus~(GSE) analogs, and its present-day properties. We find that galaxies with a GSE analog have lower star formation rates, more compact disks, and more spherical stellar halos. Crucially, significant halo-to-halo scatter remains, demonstrating that matching more than the most significant events in the Milky Way's past is necessary to recover its present-day properties. Our results highlight the necessity for large statistical samples to disentangle the stochastic nature of galaxy formation and robustly model the Milky Way's unique history.

# Notes
## Abstract
- Expect chemical compositions between planets and host stars to be linked
- Clear correlation between rocky planet interiors and host stars has not been found for FGK dwarfs and non-existent for M dwarfs
	- For M because cool stars lack detailed chemical info
- Find planets densities exhibit strong inverse relationship to $[\mathrm{MG/Fe}]$ abundances of FGK hosts
- Possible that Earth-like planet compositions vary significantly across different regions in the Galaxy
## Main Paper
### Introduction
- Since planets and stars form from the same nebular material expect abundances of solid, rock forming element to be roughly commensurate
- If there are diverse formation pathways/chemical processing expect compositions of exoplanets to not reflect those of their stars
- Hard to build a rocky planet sample with high quality density measurements
	- New RV/transit surveys are helping
- Also hard to constraint host star compositions
	- Especially for M-dwarfs
### Discussion
#### Planet formation in the Thin vs. Thick Disk
- Found a significant correlation between the host star $[Mg/Fe]$ and rocky planet bulk density
	- Could indicate differences in rocky planet compositions depending where in the galaxy they formed
	- Thin vs thick disk are characterized by lower and higher $[\alpha/Fe]$ enrichment of which $\mathrm{Mg}$ is an $\alpha$ element
	- Only see tentative to no correlation with other $\alpha$ elements like Si or Ca
		- Could be because Mg is the purest $\alpha$ element
		- Only created in core-collapse supernova
- Overall the sample is very small with only one high confidence thick disk and one potential thin disk
- Planet location in the thin/thick disks could influence. secondary atmosphere formation
#### Rocky Planets Around M Dwarfs
- Constraining M dwarf chemical compositions is non trivial
- Source data inferred from a data-driven approach [[behmardLinkRockyPlanet2025]]
	- Only get 4 possible planets that meet sample criteria
- Generally M-dwarf rocky planets are less dense but not sure if this is systematic
#### Are Rocky Exoplanet Compositions Earth-like
- Assuming rocky planets have broadly Earth-like compositions then 
	- Find rocky planets form with larger dense iron-dominated cores in more Fe-rich regions (thin disk) 
	- Are more silicate-mantle dominated in thick disk since $\alpha$ elements are more abundant than Fe
- Hints that rocky exoplanet compositions may deviate from Earth-like

> Planets and their host stars form from the same cloud of gas and dust, so we assume that their chemical compositions are linked. However, a clear correlation between rocky planet interior properties and host star chemistry remains elusive for planets around FGK dwarfs, and non-existent for planets around M dwarfs because cool stars frequently lack detailed chemical information. Here, we investigate the relationship between small (R$_{P}$ $\leq$ 1.8 R$_{\oplus}$) planet densities and host star elemental abundances. We use the Sloan Digital Sky Survey-V/Milky Way Mapper and an accompanying data-driven framework to obtain abundances for FGK and M dwarf hosts of 22 rocky planets. We find that planet densities exhibit a strong, inverse relationship to [Mg/Fe] abundances of FGK hosts (p = 0.001). This correlation becomes more significant with the addition of M dwarf hosts (p = 0.0005). If we assume that rocky planets have terrestrial-like compositions, this suggests that low [Mg/Fe] environments form planets with larger Fe-rich cores and thus higher densities. The thick disk planets in our sample help anchor this trend, illustrating the importance of sampling exoplanet properties across a range of host star populations. This finding highlights the connection between Galactic chemical evolution and rocky planet formation, and indicates that Earth-like planet compositions may vary significantly across different regions of the Galaxy.

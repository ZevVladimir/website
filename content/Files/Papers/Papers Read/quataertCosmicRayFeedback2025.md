## Main Paper
### Introduction
- Without ongoing star formation in a reservoir of cool gas require a heating mechanism to balance cooling of the hot $(T\sim10^{7-8}\mathrm{K})$ X-ray emitting plasma
	- Stellar feedback is insufficient
	- Generally believed feedback from massive BHs is what is the primary mechanism
		- Jets from AGN heat the ambient halo gas
		- Likely combination of mechanical feedback (bubbles, turbulence, waves from the get) and/or relativistic particles (CRs)
- The tension between $S_{8}=\sigma_{8}(\Omega_{m}/0.3)^{1/2}$ between CMB and weak lensing could be resolved if feedback in outskirts of group-mass halos is significantly stronger than in simulations
### Cosmic Ray Feedback
- CRs come from
	- Star formation primarily from core-collapse supernovae $(\sim10\%)$
	- BH growth
		- AGN driven winds shocking with gas
		- Relativistic jets
- Impact of CRs in massive halos depends on their spatial distribution which depends on *uncertain* physics of CR transport
- From observations implication that cosmic-rays have not had a large dynamical effect on the baryon distribution at the virial radius in massive halos
### Discussion/Conclusion
- Argued that CRs produced by BH accretion during primary epoch oF BH growth $(z\sim1-3)$ must be a key source of feedback in massive halos
- At lower redshifts CR feedback is likely to preferentially act in the outskirts of $\sim10^{13-15}M_{\odot}$ halos but not in the outskirts of yet more massive galaxy clusters
- Properties of CRs are sensitive to entire galaxy history not current BH activity as their transport time scales are $\gtrsim\mathrm{Gyr}$
- CRs may be a key mechanism in modifying the baryon distribution in group mass systems
- A large dynamically important CR pressure in halo outskirts at lower redshift is likely associated with a large CR pressure in the inner halo at high redshift. This could be avoided thought and need more simulations

> We use order of magnitude estimates and observational constraints to argue that feedback from relativistic cosmic rays (CRs) produced by massive black holes is likely to have a particularly large effect at radii of order the virial radius and larger in group-mass halos. We show that for a range of plausible (but uncertain) CR transport parameters and energetics, the pressure produced by CRs generated by the central massive black hole over its lifetime can be of order the thermal gas pressure in the outskirts of ∼1013−14M⊙ halos (but not in more massive clusters). The properties of this CR feedback at low redshift are not well predicted by the radiative cooling rate of hot gas at smaller radii, which is often used as a proxy for `current' black hole feedback. This is because most black hole growth happens early in massive halos, and CR transport timescales in halo outskirts are Gyr or more; the accumulated CR energy thus depends on the full history of black hole activity in the halo. The large CR pressure in group-mass systems likely leads to CR-driven outflows that move gas from large halo radii to outside the virial radius. Such feedback would not be captured by current cosmological simulations that focus on mechanical black hole feedback; in particular, CR feedback remains active even long after the mechanical feedback sourcing the CRs has turned off. We speculate that this CR feedback may be important for explaining the weak lensing S8 tension and the evidence for strong feedback at large halo radii from kinetic Sunyaev-Zeldovich measurements. Prospects for testing this mechanism observationally and implementing the necessary physics in cosmological simulations are discussed.

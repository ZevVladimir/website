# Notes
## Abstract

- Found planet GJ 12b
    - Temperate (300K)
    - Earth sized ($0.96\pm 0.05 R_\oplus$)
    - Good alternative for TRAPPIST-1 planets for atmospheric characterization
- Using RV measurements find mass to be $0.71\pm 0.12 M_\oplus$
- From the mass and radius the density is a bit less than Earth’s
- Likely has trouble retaining primary atmosphere due to low mass but with a higher eccentricity ($e\approx0.16$)

## Main Paper

- M Dwarf exoplanets produce the largest amplitude photometric signals there have yet to be any definitive atmospheric signatures of rocky M dwarf planets
- Flares complicate efforts at fitting planet properties and spots in late type stars can contaminate their planets’ transmission spectra
- Some issues in the data from a temperature anomaly that changed the zero point of observations. There were corrections made but it might not be perfect
    - In addition analyses of the data will be made including and excluding pre anomaly data
- Difficult to accurately determine the rotational period of the star due to low significance in signals as well as discrepancies in past works of the rotation period
    - But it is still a relatively long rotation period around 80-120 days explaining why it was difficult to characterize given the 150 day data range of this work
- Including chromatic offset placed at the temperature anomaly as well as with the RV slope from the MAROON-X data substantially reduces the model’s RV residuals
- Based off several models that include different signals (transiting planet 39d Keplerian, 58d Keplerian, $e=0,e\neq0$, [[Gaussian Process Model|GP]] find that:
    - There are at least 3 signals:
        - Transiting planet
        - 40d signal
        - 60d signal
    - Also find models with highest likelihoods contain 3 Keplerian signals and 1 GP signal
    - Find eccentric models are typically preferred over models with circular orbits
- Based on mass and radius appears that GJ12b has a density between a pure-rock planet and one with an earth-like composition
- Determining if GJ12b has an atmosphere would be very helpful in placing the cosmic shoreline and determine which planets are good to look at for potential habitability
- While unlikely to have a thick primordial H/He atmosphere it may have a secondary atmosphere from tidally-induced volcanism (if it does in fact have an eccentric orbit)
- Unlikely to have a water mass fraction $>10\%$ or potentially no water at all

> [!Abstract]

>

> While JWST has provided us with the opportunity to probe the atmospheres of potentially-habitable planets, observations of the TRAPPIST-1 system have shown us that active stars severely complicate efforts at studying their planets. GJ 12b is a newly-discovered temperate (Teq ~ 300 K), Earth-sized (Rp = 0.96 +/- 0.05 Earth radii) planet orbiting an inactive M dwarf that might be a good alternate to the TRAPPIST-1 planets for atmospheric characterization. In this paper, we use MAROON-X radial velocities to measure a mass of 0.71 +/- 0.12 Earth masses for GJ 12b. We also find moderate evidence that the planet has an eccentric (e ~ 0.16) orbit. GJ 12b's mass results in a planetary density comparable to or less dense than Earth, possibly indicating the presence of water or a low bulk iron mass fraction. With its low mass, GJ 12b is likely within reach of JWST transmission spectroscopy observations, making it an excellent target for determining the location of the cosmic shoreline. Its low mass may mean that the planet could have trouble retaining its primary atmosphere during the star's active pre-main-sequence phase. However, if it has a heightened eccentricity, it may be able to sustain a secondary atmosphere through tidally-induced volcanism.

# Notes
## Abstract
- [[Gaussian Process Model|GPR]]Ns allow for a way to analyze simultaneous time-series combining structural properties of Bayesian NNs with non-parametric flexibility of GPs
- Demonstrate that this framework can jointly model RV data and traditional stellar activity indicators
## Main Paper
### Introduction
- GP covariance function describes the behavior of the stellar RV contribution
- The mean function is assumed to be generated by whatever planetary signals may be present
### Gaussian Process Regression Networks
- [[rajpaulGaussianProcessFramework2015]] proposes framework to jointly model RVs and several activity indicators
	- Extend method from [[0aigrainSimpleMethodEstimate2012]] which relates activity signal expected in RVs to drop in flux caused by point like spots (F) and its first time derivative (F')
	- Modeled F with a latent [[Gaussian Process Model|GP]] and derived necessary exppresions for a multi output model based on linear combinations of F and F'
	- Further extensions are limited by their inability to tackle the non-stationary behavior of stellar magnetic activity as a function of time
#### Gaussian Process
- A GP is sufficiently flexible to model the [[Quasi-periodic kernel]]  structure with a kernel while taking into account Keplerian contributions through its mean function
- Marginal likelihood can be maximized to obtain a set of the 'best' hyper-parameters compatible with the data
#### GPRN model for stellar activity
- Jointly modeling RVs and activity indicators can be frames as a multi-output regression problem
- GPRNs can 
	- Capture input-dependent highly non-linear correlations between outputs
	- Provide heavy-tailed predictive distributions
	- Resist overfitting
- Model for a set of $P$ outputs $y(t)$
	- $y(t)=W(t)\left[ f(t)+\sigma_{f}\epsilon(t) \right]+\sigma_{y}z(t)$
	- $f(t)$ is a small set of Q latent functions *nodes*
		- $f_{j}(t)\sim \mathcal{GP}(0,k_{f_{j}});j=1,\dots,Q$
	- $W(t)$ is a $P\times Q$ projection matrix of *weight* functions
		- $W_{ij}(t)\sim \mathcal{GP}(0,k_{q_{ij}});i=1,\dots,P;j=1,\dots ,Q$
	- $\epsilon(t)$ and $z(t)$ are independent white noise processes
- GPRN creates a non-stationary linear combination of independent GPs
	- Correlations between variables are modeled by latent functions $f(t)$
	- Final amplitudes are given by projection through the weight functions $W(t)$
- GPRN can be used to model complex non-periodic signals with different decaying timescales and non-stationary amplitudes
### Results
#### Interpretation of results
- Can recover synodic solar rotation period when time series are analyzed both individually and jointly
	- Overall MAP values for GPRN hyper-parameters are very similar
	- This suggests RVs and activity indicators share same type of temporal evolution
- GPRN describes the behavior of each time series individually very well
	- Substantial reductions in rms and significance of periodogram peaks
- Reasoning for why jointly modeled performs worse
	- RVs lead activity indicators by 1-2 days
	- But since the model used only has one node can't accurately capture this
- Both individual and joint analysis lead to preferred posterior predicted means for weight functions that decrease over time span
	- This is connected to the expected diminishing of solar activity
## Conclusion
- Present new framework to model RV observations in combination with activity indicators
- GPRN allows for modeling non-stationary outputs
- Joint analysis of RVs and an activity indicator leads to significantly worse description of RV variations with time
	- But inclusion of extra nodes could improve this
	- Could benefit from the inclusion of a second node with a kernel that is the derivative of the kernel in the first node

> Stellar photospheric activity is known to limit the detection and characterisation of extra-solar planets. In particular, the study of Earth-like planets around Sun-like stars requires data analysis methods that can accurately model the stellar activity phenomena affecting radial velocity (RV) measurements. Gaussian Process Regression Networks (GPRNs) offer a principled approach to the analysis of simultaneous time-series, combining the structural properties of Bayesian neural networks with the non-parametric flexibility of Gaussian Processes. Using HARPS-N solar spectroscopic observations encompassing three years, we demonstrate that this framework is capable of jointly modelling RV data and traditional stellar activity indicators. Although we consider only the simplest GPRN configuration, we are able to describe the behaviour of solar RV data at least as accurately as previously published methods. We confirm the correlation between the RV and stellar activity time series reaches a maximum at separations of a few days, and find evidence of non-stationary behaviour in the time series, associated with an approaching solar activity minimum.

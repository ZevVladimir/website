# Notes
## Abstract
- Study the effects of passage of a Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) mass satellite on the distance end velocity distributions of satellites in a $\Lambda$+Cold Dark Matter simulations of Milky Way (MW) sized halos
- FInd
	- Velocity distribution develops high velocity tail
	- After correcting velocity distribution it is consistent with distributions in halos of masses $8\times10^{11}M_{\odot}<M_{\mathrm{200c}}<1.5\times10^{12}M_{\odot}$
- Present new halo mass estimator
	- $M_{\mathrm{200c}}=c\sigma_{\mathrm{3D}}^{2}r_{\mathrm{med}}$
		- $c$ is the coefficient calibrated using satellite systems in simulated MW-sized halos
		- $\sigma_{\mathrm{3D}}^{2}$ is the variance of 3D velocities 
		- $r_{\mathrm{med}}$ is the median halocentric distance of the satellites
	- Has only $s=8\%$ scatter around median relation of estimated and true halo masses
	- Deviates $<2s$ from median during pericentric passage of LMC subhalo
- Estimate virial mass of MW: $M_{\mathrm{200c}}=9.96\pm1.45\times10^{11}M_{\odot}$
## Main Paper
### Introduction
- Velocities of galaxies have been used to
	- Infer information about the mass of 
		- Virialized objects (galaxy groups and clusters)
		- Unvirialized outskirts of such objects
		- Gravitationally interacting unvirialized pairs of galaxies
	- Constrain the masses of the galaxies they are moving through
- Improved velocity measurements and analyses has reduced uncertainties between halo masses for the MW
- One systematic uncertainty is the effect of the LMC on the orbits and velocities of dwarf satellites and globular clusters which are used as tracers and the MW itself
	- Induces a uniform reflex motion of the MW disk relative to the outer halo and distorts distribution of velocities
- Here explore effects of the LMC on the velocity distribution of satellite systems
	- Use Caterpillar suite of high-resolution simulations of MW sized halos
## Conclusion
- Find that the effect of the passage of an LMC-like satellite on the velocity distribution is substantial
	- Velocity distribution develops a high-$v$ tail due to the motions induced by LMC
	- Satellite spatial distribution becomes more centrally concentrated
- Correcting for the effect of the LMC on the 3D velocity distribution favors halo masses in range $1-1.3\times10^{12}M_{\odot}$
	- But velocity distribution is a low accuracy estimator of halo mass
- Consider distribution of specific angular momenta $j$ as an estimator of mass and confirm it isn't sensitive to the passage of LMC-mass satellites
	- Also $j$ distribution is a weak function of host halo mass and poor mass indicator
- Present new halo mass estimate proportional to variance of 3D velocities
- Show velocity variance and median distance deviate in opposite directions during pericentric passage of LMC satellites
- Apply estimator to MW satellite system and get estimate of viral mass of MW

> We present a study illustrating the effects of the passage of a Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) mass satellite on the distance and velocity distributions of satellites in Λ+Cold Dark Matter simulations of Milky Way (MW) sized halos. In agreement with previous studies, we find that during such a passage the velocity distribution develops a high-velocity tail, which can bias velocity-based virial halo mass estimates. When the velocity distribution of MW satellites is corrected for effects of the LMC passage, it is consistent with the distributions in halos of masses as low as and as high as 1.5×1012M⊙. We present a new halo mass estimator , where c is the coefficient calibrated using satellite systems in the simulated MW-sized halos, is the variance of 3D velocities taken with the sign of the radial velocity of each satellite, and is the median halocentric distance of the satellites. We show that the estimator has only s=8% scatter around the median relation of the estimated and true halo masses and deviates by <2s from the median during the pericentric passage of an LMC-like subhalo. This is because and deviate in the opposite directions during such passages. We apply the estimator to the MW satellite system and estimate the virial mass of the Milky Way of , in good agreement with several recent estimates using other methods.

- Model star-by-star chemical enrichment and galaxy formation in early universe
- 1 pc resolution
- Model galaxy evolution in first $\sim300\mathrm{Myr}$ with individual stars and feedback processes
# Introduction
- Understanding early star/galaxy formation is through studying stellar chemical abundances in ancient stars
	- Preserve information about 
		- Pop $\mathrm{III}$ and $\mathrm{II}$ star formation
		- Hierarchical galaxy assembly
		- Nucleosynthetic processes
- There has been abundance scatter found in elements within and between dwarf galaxies
	- Imply presence of complex galaxy formation processes
		- Source- and time-dependent metal mixing
		- Hierarchical galaxy merging
		- Bursty star formation
- Hydrodynamic galaxy sims with parsec resolution and star particles as individual stars
	- Captures variations in when/where/how individual stars inject energy and metals
	- Can investigate full chemical abundance distributions
	- Better understand how individual feedback affects galaxy evolution 
- AEOS project aim to provide insight into
	- Metal mixing within the ISM at high $z$
	- Origins of the spreads in observed stellar chemical abundances
	- How to develop physically motivated prescriptions for inhomogeneous mixing/abundance scatter
	- Identification of signatures of stars formed from Pop $\mathrm{III}$ enrichment
	- The role of $\alpha-$ and $s-$process elements in constraining star formation timescales and stellar ages
	- Impact of adopting star-by-star approach to star formation in simulations
# Conclusion
- Introduce AEOS project which traces early galaxy formation and models chemical enrichment of individual stars
	- Pop III enrichment
	- Radiative transfer
	- Cosmological galaxy formation
- Capture interplay between galaxy evolution and different nucleosynthetic processes
- Demonstrate that early galaxies are better treated as systems that evolve together
	- So small that gas is easily ejected and mixes

> The AEOS project introduces a series of high-resolution cosmological simulations that model star-by-star chemical enrichment and galaxy formation in the early Universe, achieving 1 pc resolution. These simulations capture the complexities of galaxy evolution within the first ~300 Myr by modeling individual stars and their feedback processes. By incorporating chemical yields from individual stars, AEOS generates galaxies with diverse stellar chemical abundances, linking them to hierarchical galaxy formation and early nucleosynthetic events. These simulations underscore the importance of chemical abundance patterns in ancient stars as vital probes of early nucleosynthesis, star formation histories, and galaxy formation. We examine the metallicity floors of various elements resulting from Pop III enrichment, providing best-fit values for eight different metals (e.g., [O/H] = -4.0) to guide simulations without Pop III models. Additionally, we identify galaxies that begin star formation with Pop II after external enrichment and investigate the frequency of CEMP stars at varying metallicities. The AEOS simulations offer detailed insights into the relationship between star formation, feedback, and chemical enrichment. Future work will extend these simulations to later epochs to interpret the diverse stellar populations of the Milky Way and its satellites.

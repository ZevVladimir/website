1. The orbit of a planet is an ellipse with the sun at one focus
2. The line joining a planet and the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal intervals of time
3. The squares of the orbital periods of the planets are proportional to the cubes of their semi-major axes
	- General 2 body problem: $P^2=\dfrac{4\pi^2}{GM}a^3$
	- Motion of planet relative to star *not* the barycenter found by applying acceleration to system canceling that of the star $\dfrac{GM_{p}}{r^2}$ with r being the separation. Then $P^2=\dfrac{4\pi^2}{G(M_{\star}+M_{p})a^3_{rel}}$
	- Orbit of star around system barycenter: $P^2=\dfrac{4\pi^2}{GM'}a_{\star}^3$ with $M'=\dfrac{M_{p^3}}{(M_{\star}+M_{p})^2}$ 
	- Following are in proportion
		- $a_{\star}:a_{p}:a_{rel}=M_{p}:M_{\star}:(M_{\star}+M_{p})$
			- $a_{rel}=a_{\star}+a_{p}$
		- $e_{rel}=e_{\star}=e_{p},\:P_{rel}=P_{\star}=P_{p}$, the three orbits are coplanar and the orientations of the two barycentric orbits $\omega$ differ by $180 \degree$

## Research Problem
500 words, Describe (in one or two paragraphs) a science problem that you encountered during your research experience or course work, and discuss how you solved that particular problem.

---

One research problem I encountered was for my project with Dr. Benedikt Diemer to implement a machine learning model to classify whether dark matter particles where orbiting or infalling into a halo in simulations. When we scaled up our model to incorporate data from six large simulations, the dataset expanded from gigabytes to terabytes, presenting significant challenges in managing and processing the increased volume on my personal computer. So I transferred my code and the data to Zaratan, UMD’s supercomputer, and began the process of learning how to work with a high-performance computer. However, when training this large dataset additional issues arose with managing memory and effectively distributing the workload to train the model efficiently. With additional research I found Dask, a python package that allows for easy parallelization. Feeling confident, I quickly changed my code to use Dask methods and structures and tried to train a model. But when I attempted to implement GPUs into my parallelization once again everything fell apart and I was left searching for possible solutions. As I was using packages that members of my research group did not have experience with I first turned to the Zaratan IT team as the issue was related to how Zaratan’s architecture was interacting with Dask. Working with one of the team’s members I began to investigate the issue. I first experimented with alternative implementations and narrowed the problem down to how Dask was assigning CPUs to its workers with GPUs. With this new information I turned to the Dask forums where I talked with one of the developers of Dask to resolve this issue. Working with them I was able to slightly modify the underlying code in Dask to correct the implementation to allow my code to run correctly with GPUs.

## Research Projects
500 words, Provide details for any research projects that you worked on in the past 5 years that includes a short project description, duration and advisor.

---

Since November, 2022 I have worked with Dr. Benedikt Diemer at the University of Maryland to train a machine learning model to improve a dynamical definition of dark matter halos. The dynamical definition of the halo is relatively new and does not use a radius to define the halo’s limits and instead the halo is defined by the particles that are orbiting the halo’s potential. Machine learning was chosen as a new approach to determine which particles are orbiting as it can take complex information and provide fast and accurate classifications all while using a smaller dataset. With our model we achieved 97% accuracy in our classifications, but more importantly we accurately reproduced the density profiles across different halo mass bins and accurately classified particles in highly dense areas of the model’s phase space.

From May, 2022 to December, 2023 I worked with Dr. Shabnam Jabeen with Quantum Machine Learning (QML). We worked with implementing different quantum machine learning models with astrophysical datasets. Using a Variational Quantum Regressor from Qiskit we examined how well it compared to classical machine learning in identifying the importance of different halo parameters in the galaxy-halo connection. I also use a quantum random forest to compare with my results from my work with Dr. Benedikt Diemer. Overall, we found that the QML methods showed promise but struggled with handling the large datasets required and due to this generally had lower accuracy.

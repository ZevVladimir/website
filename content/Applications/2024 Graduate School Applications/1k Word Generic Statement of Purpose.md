As a sophomore, I began my first independent research project with Dr. Shabnam Jabeen at the University of Maryland (UMD) as part of the First-year Innovation and Research Experience’s Quantum Machine Learning (QML) stream.  I chose to compare QML with machine learning (ML) in exploring the galaxy-halo connection due to the well-defined relationship between the input parameters and outputs. I reached out to Dr. Ana Delgado, then at Harvard, who had previously used ML to explore this problem. I also contacted Dr. Benedikt Diemer at UMD, an expert in dark matter halos, for direct guidance in developing this astrophysical project. Over the next year, I developed a robust comparison method to identify which characteristics of halos most effectively model the connection between halos and galaxies. I presented my findings at UMD's Undergraduate Research Day and look forward to continuing to use QML as an astrophysical analysis tool.

As I finalized my project with QML, I approached Dr. Diemer to collaborate on a new project, where he proposed using ML to improve the classification of dark matter particles in a simulation. With my research into potential machine learning models, we decided to use XGBoost. I had found it easy to implement, scalable for large datasets, and interpretable—qualities essential for our goals. I trained the model using billions of particles from multiple simulations, which quickly overwhelmed my computer. I pivoted to using UMD’s supercomputer, Zaratan, and used specialized libraries to distribute the computational workloads.  Although my model produced very accurate particle classifications, I wanted to dive deeper. I analyzed regions of interest using phase space plots, density profiles, and Shapley values (SHAP). With these techniques, I demonstrated how ML can also be used to gain insight into the astrophysical processes within a halo. This work culminated in my successful senior thesis defense, for which I received high honors. I will submit a first-author paper to the Astrophysical Journal and recently presented a poster at the Cosmology and Galaxy Astrophysics with Simulations and Machine Learning 2024 Conference hosted by the Simons Foundation.

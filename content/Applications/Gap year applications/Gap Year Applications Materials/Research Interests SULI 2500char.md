At Oak Ridge National Laboratory, I am especially interested in how astrophysics, high-performance computing, and data science are all combined to perform high level research. Through my courses and research, I’ve seen how large-scale astronomy simulations can reveal deep insights into everything from galaxy evolution to stellar dynamics. ORNL’s strengths in exascale computing, data-intensive science, and using AI to accelerate simulations and analysis take that kind of work to the next level, applying it across a wide range of fields with even greater scale and precision.

My undergraduate honors thesis focused on using machine learning to redefine dark matter halos based on dynamical criteria, and I developed a complete pipeline for training and analyzing a machine learning model on N-body simulation data. ORNL’s emphasis on advanced computing, as seen in projects like ExaSky and its application of AI to physics, aligns closely with my experience and aspirations. I hope to contribute to the development of tools that both accelerate and interpret complex simulations, particularly in projects where physical insight and computational rigor must work in tandem.

I also chose Brookhaven National Laboratory due to its strength in both experimental and theoretical physics, especially its work in developing novel computational methods for ML, high-performance computing, and its involvement in the Rubin Observatory’s LSST. I am drawn to BNL’s quantum computing efforts to improve noisy intermediate-scale quantum computers to apply to real-world problems. My prior experience with Dr. Shabnam Jabeen at the University of Maryland directly connects with this as we developed quantum machine learning models to analyze the galaxy-dark matter halo connection.

Both labs appeal to me due to their interdisciplinary integration of physics, computation, and AI—fields I intend to pursue in graduate school. ORNL stands out for its computing infrastructure and simulation-heavy projects, which match my background in theoretical and computational astrophysics. BNL provides similar opportunities to develop my skills with high-performance computing and push the current boundaries with AI but also to develop new quantum computing algorithms and simulations.

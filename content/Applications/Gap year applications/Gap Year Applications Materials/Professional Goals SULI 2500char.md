My long-term goal is to pursue a Ph.D. in computational physics, with a focus on utilizing machine learning and high-performance computing to better understand complex systems whether they are astronomical or biological. Following a Ph.D., I aspire to contribute to scientific research at a national lab or university, where I can work on solving computational problems to further scientific understand and answering fundamental questions while mentoring future scientists.

While I was an undergraduate, I have sought out opportunities that combine physics, computation, and data science. My honors thesis focused on using machine learning to refine the definition of dark matter halos in N-body simulations by dynamically classifying particles as orbiting or infalling, reducing the need for arbitrary radial boundaries. I built a full analysis pipeline that utilized the University of Maryland's supercomputer, Zaratan, to process billions of datapoints with multiprocessing and train a machine learning model with GPUs that achieved 97% accuracy. I also analyzed the model's decisions using SHAP values, and compared my model to several other alternative methods. During this process, I developed a love for using and developing computational tools to utilize complex data and gain insights that otherwise wouldn't be possible.

The SULI program presents a unique opportunity for me to further develop these skills under some of the best research scientists in the world. Working at a Department of Energy national laboratory would allow me to strengthen my foundation in scientific computing through contributing to high-fidelity simulations, developing interpretable machine learning models, or working with large-scale datasets. In addition, SULI provides an excellent opportunity to utilize and learn about projects like the Frontier supercomputer or the Vera C. Rubin Observatory which are on the cutting edge of what is being developed now.

I see SULI as an excellent bridge between my undergraduate work and my goals of achieving a Ph.D. and working in research. It would provide me with invaluable mentorship, exposure to a multidisciplinary research environment, and the opportunity to explore how physics-based thinking and computational tools can come together to solve complex, real-world problems across many different fields.
